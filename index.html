What really controls a small weir?

A weir can look like a simple section across a stream. The research becomes more interesting when one change begins to move head, unit discharge, jump behaviour, material demand, stability and foundation response at the same time.

One change travels through the whole system

Discharge, crest length and coefficient determine head. Head and unit discharge shape the downstream hydraulic state. Geometry affects material demand and stability, while foundation class changes the scour question.

The first research task was therefore to separate independent inputs from quantities that are mathematically derived.

04

Head is a balance, not a single driver

H = [Q/(C·L)]2/3. More discharge raises head; more overflow length spreads the same discharge and lowers it. The exact elasticities are +2/3 for Q and −2/3 for L and C.

But Q/L also determines unit discharge, so the head relationship leads directly into downstream forcing.

05

Forcing rises faster than it first appears

Across the eligible formal-jump domain, the fitted response follows P′ ∝ q1.67 Fr11.87 with R² ≈ 0.9999. A 10% rise in q corresponds to about 17% more forcing; a 10% rise in Fr1 corresponds to about 19.5% more, holding the other fixed.

Hydraulic response becomes sharper — but reducing forcing cannot be judged without looking at geometry and material demand.

06

Why height quickly becomes a material penalty

The body-area proxy follows A = P·T + 0.5·s·P². Height enters quadratically through the downstream batter term, so material demand grows nonlinearly as sections become taller.

Material economy alone still cannot decide the section, because stability follows a different multivariable pattern.

07

Stability does not follow one simple rule

In the applicability-conditioned diagnostic regression, cohesion is strongly favourable, while height is persistently adverse; downstream slope, discharge and tailwater also contribute. The pooled R² is about 0.876.

Important. These are diagnostic multivariable coefficients, not a universal field factor-of-safety equation.

08

There is no single optimum weir

Among 2,585 eligible competent-rock cases, 101 were nondominated when material proxy, forcing, tailwater mismatch and rock threshold were minimized together. None is a scalar winner.

The durable finding is the trade-off structure; exact Pareto membership remains sample-sensitive.

10

High forcing does not automatically mean greater jump-development demand

Unit discharge can drive forcing strongly even where normalized jump-development metrics remain similar at comparable Froude number. Forcing and dissipation-development demand are related, but they are not the same axis.

Reference only. The jump-development lengths in this research are not final IS 4997:2026 stilling-basin dimensions.

11

Scour is where the models begin to disagree

Bormann–Julien (BJ) and D’Agostino–Ferro (DAF) overlap in 14,369 synthetic states, yet their agreement is only partial: median DAF/BJ ≈ 1.107, Spearman ρ ≈ 0.422, and about 50.3% of cases fall within a factor of two.

The disagreement is structured, not random — so the next task is to describe that uncertainty, not average it away.

What this research can — and cannot — say

What it establishes

How selected hydraulic, geometric, stability and foundation-related responses behave across the frozen synthetic research domain; how trade-offs recur; where models disagree; and which variables matter under the stated analysis.

What remains outside the claim

Site validation, construction-ready dimensions, final IS 4997:2026 basin provisions, a universal alluvial scour law, and a final rock/foundation-depth method for field release.

16

Methods, data, references and author

Synthetic experiment

Baseline reasoning, one-at-a-time tests, pairwise response surfaces, four 50,000-scenario LHS replications, eligibility filtering, Pareto analysis and normalized families.

Global sensitivity

Dedicated scrambled Sobol/Jansen analysis with base N = 8,192 on validated response surrogates. Stability is intentionally excluded from unconditional Sobol ranking.

Scour-model comparison

BJ and DAF are compared only in the common valid synthetic overlap. The compact envelope models disagreement between them; it is not a physical scour equation.

Data provenance

Authoritative source tables remain build inputs. The public article uses compact render-ready JSON generated from the frozen analysis; original PNG/JPG figures are visual references only, never numerical data sources.
